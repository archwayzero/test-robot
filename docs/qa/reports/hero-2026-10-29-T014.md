## 3. In-engine measurements per clip
Method: AnimationPlayer `seek(t, true)` at 30 fps over every frame 0..N. Headless Godot does not expose skinned vertices,
so the test **CPU-skins every vertex** from the imported mesh arrays, Skin bind poses and `Skeleton3D` global bone poses.
This is the same linear-blend formula the renderer uses. At rest it reproduces the rest AABB.

Limits: stand clips <= 1.85 m (FULL clearance 2.2), crouch <= 1.25 (CROUCH ceiling 1.3), crawl <= 0.70
(CRAWL ceiling 0.75), lowest point >= -0.01. Loop seam, two checks per looping clip:
(a) **key seam**: for every track, the value at time `length` vs the value at 0, read from the Animation resource with
`track_get_key_value` (or `rotation/position/scale_track_interpolate` where a track has no key at that time), not with
`seek()`. Limits per track: rotation <= 0.25 deg, position <= 1 mm, scale <= 0.001. Tracks of any other type fail the
test. (b) **wrap step**: the max vertex step from the last sampled frame before `length` (frame N-1) to frame 0 must be
<= 2x the median frame-to-frame step.

Correction (review finding): the first version of this report measured the seam as pose(seek(length)) vs pose(0). On a
LOOP_LINEAR clip, Godot 4.7.2 wraps `seek(length)` to 0.0, so that value was always 0 and could never fail. It is
replaced by (a) and (b). For loops, frame N is now set to frame 0 explicitly instead of being sampled with seek.
**The checks can fail** (`test_seam_check_detects_broken_loop`): a copy of walk_loop with its last three hips rotation
keys (time >= length - 0.07 s, including the key at `length`) turned 30 deg fails both checks: key seam 30.00 deg on
`Armature/Skeleton3D:hips`, and wrap step 492 mm vs 2x median 109 mm.

How to read the table:
- **Seams.** In every loop, each multi-key track has a key at `length` (0 tracks rely on interpolation wrap). Those
  keys equal the keys at 0 (largest difference 0.04 deg, walk_loop). Single-key tracks are constant. The wrap step is
  at most 1.7x the median (crouch_idle_loop: 7.8 vs 4.5 mm, a slow idle where every step is a few mm). The per-frame
  step series runs smoothly through the wrap (walk: `... 81 53 53 | 55 55 61 ...` mm), so there is no hitch or pop at
  the loop point. In the locomotion loops the wrap step is below the median because the seams sit at the slow
  (passing) phase of each cycle. Margin to watch: the idle loops are at 1.4x and 1.7x of a 2x limit.
- **Planted blade speed** is the median speed along +Z (backward) of blade vertices that touch the floor (y < 15 mm) in
  two consecutive frames. These are material points, so it measures true slip, not the rolling contact patch. All four
  locomotion clips match the game speed within 1 %, so **no foot sliding** at WALK 1.6, RUN 4.0, CROUCH 1.0 and CRAWL
  0.5 m/s. Min..max per clip: walk 1.55..1.62, jog 3.53..4.00, crouch walk 0.98..1.01, crawl 0.29..0.53. Crawl has one
  short phase where a blade drags at ~0.3 m/s. The rolling contact patch moves more slowly (walk median 1.45), which is
  expected for a curved hook foot. Lateral drift reaches up to 0.11-0.12 m/s in walk and crawl at single frames, which
  is not visible in the captures. The test asserts within 10 % (0.05 m/s for idles).
- **Jump vs physics.** Physics airtime = 2 x 4.0 / 9.8 = **0.816 s**. In the clip both blades leave the floor at frame 1
  (0.033 s) and touch down at frame 24-25 (**0.80-0.83 s**). The highest tuck is 0.32 m at frame 11. The landing
  compression (top dips to 1.56 m at frame 29) and the recovery play after the physics `landed`. The test asserts
  lift-off <= 0.1 s and touchdown within 0.1 s of 0.816 s. Starting the clip at frame 0 on `jumped()` lines up well.
- **Jump end pose** matches idle_loop frame 0 within 21 mm. **Jump start** differs from idle frame 0 by up to
  229 mm (arms already forward and legs extending: takeoff pose), and from crouch_idle by 640 mm.
- **Reach.** This is the horizontal extent of the mesh from the capsule axis. The capsule radius is 0.3 and E5 keeps the
  capsule >= 0.29 m from walls. Facing a wall, the mesh will therefore enter it by up to about 0.2 m (walk), 0.3 m (jog,
  crouch walk) and **0.55 m (crawl forward) or 0.44 m (crawl backward)**.

## 7. Notes for T019 (player module, `module-dev`)
1. **Jump blend-in:** the jump starts in its takeoff pose, up to 23 cm away from idle (arms). Crossfade into `jump` over
   about 0.1 s, or accept a small arm pop. Do not delay the clip: its air section already matches the physics airtime
   when it starts at frame 0 on `jumped()`. Blend out to idle_loop freely (end pose within 2 cm of idle frame 0).
2. **Locomotion playback rate:** the clips are authored for exactly 1.6 / 4.0 / 1.0 / 0.5 m/s. If movement speed varies
   (analogue stick between 0 and RUN_THRESHOLD), scale playback by `actual_speed / clip_speed` so the blades stay
   planted.
3. **Wall clipping:** the mesh reaches beyond the 0.3 m capsule (crawl forward 0.85 m, back 0.74 m; jog 0.60 m). Facing
   a wall, the head, hands or blades will enter the wall geometry. This is expected with a capsule controller. Mention
   it in the M2 video review rather than changing the capsule.
4. **Low-ceiling margins are small:** crawl top 0.694 m (ceiling 0.75) and crouch walk top 1.233 m (ceiling 1.3).
   Additive bobbing or blend overshoot in the AnimationTree could push the hood or backpack into the ceiling insert.
   Keep blends between same-stance clips.
5. **Crawl camera:** with the 1.2 m arm the blades reach back toward the camera and fill the lower third of the frame.
   T030's fade-when-close rule (< ~0.6 m) may trigger on the blade tips rather than the body. Check this in the M2
   stances video.
